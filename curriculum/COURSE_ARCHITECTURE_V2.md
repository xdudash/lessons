# SlovakGo — Course Architecture

## ACTIVE MODEL

SlovakGo is a complete Slovak CEFR A1–C2 course.

**Lesson count is an output of curriculum design, never a predefined input.**

Retired quotas:

- 10,000 total lessons;
- 1,000 A1 lessons;
- 40 lessons per section;
- any requirement to fill numerical slots.

These may exist in historical audits but must never drive generation.

## CEFR progression

### A1 — Forms and fundamentals
Basic high-frequency structures, core vocabulary, simple interaction, immediate practical communication.

### A2 — Systems and controlled expansion
Broader grammatical systems, routine narration, controlled combinations, wider everyday functions.

### B1 — Connections and independent communication
Connected discourse, explanations, experiences, plans, reasons, problem solving and increasing independence.

### B2 — Composition and complex communication
Complex syntax, argumentation, register awareness, composition and flexible interaction.

### C1 — Choice and precision
Fine-grained lexical/grammatical choice, discourse organization, register, implication and precision.

### C2 — Control and nuance
Near-complete flexibility, stylistic control, subtle distinctions, idiomaticity, inference and adaptation.

## Mastery model

Curriculum progression is:

**NEW → STABILIZE → CONTRAST → TRANSFER → INTEGRATE → MASTERY**

A target may move through these stages more than once and may require different amounts of practice.

These are functions, not fixed lesson numbers.

## Section design

Every section must define:

1. learning outcomes;
2. linguistic targets;
3. communicative functions;
4. vocabulary/expressions;
5. prerequisites;
6. review dependencies;
7. transfer opportunities;
8. integration requirements;
9. mastery gate.

Only after this definition is the number of lessons chosen.

## Production batch

Production uses **3 coherent consecutive sections per batch**.

Each selected section normally receives **3–6 lessons** in the initial batch when production is required.

Three is the minimum starting depth. Six is the maximum for that batch, not a quota. If evidence later shows that more practice is needed, a later batch may extend the section.

## Complete lesson architecture

A strong lesson normally follows:

**TARGET → THEORY → EXAMPLES → VOCABULARY → CONTROLLED PRACTICE → CONTRAST → CONTEXT → PRODUCTION → COMMUNICATION → REAL LIFE → MASTERY EVIDENCE**

The exact order may vary when pedagogy requires it.

## Gold standard

The canonical `A1-S01-L01` is the quality reference. It demonstrates:

- complete lesson envelope;
- 2 theory screens;
- 6 vocabulary items;
- 16 exercises;
- varied exercise mechanics;
- contextual meaning;
- grammar/vocabulary/reading/dialogue/writing practice;
- natural phrases;
- multi-step final situation;
- localization;
- completion/result structure.

The lesson's exact content must not be copied.

## Functional exposure vs mastery

A useful expression may contain grammar that is formally taught later. That is allowed as functional exposure.

Do not claim mastery of a later grammatical system merely because a phrase containing it appeared earlier.

## Existing content

Existing lessons are evidence and source material. They must be mapped to current targets and audited for quality.

A structural parse does not prove pedagogical quality.

## Vocabulary ownership

Vocabulary repetition is valid when it serves review, transfer or mastery. Accidental duplication is not.

Use target ownership to determine whether a word/expression is:

- NEW;
- REVIEW;
- TRANSFER;
- MASTERY.

Frequency alone does not prove mastery.

## Section completion gate

Do not mark a section complete until:

- all declared outcomes are covered;
- prerequisites are respected;
- core vocabulary/functions are usable;
- review and transfer are present where required;
- integrated use is demonstrated;
- mastery evidence is sufficient.

## No filler

If one lesson can achieve the defined outcome properly, do not create three merely because three is the minimum production starting point. The minimum applies only when multiple lessons are pedagogically required.

Every lesson must have a distinct learning purpose.
